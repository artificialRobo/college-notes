# Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (AI)

## Definition 

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science that focuses on designing systems capable of performing tasks that normally require human intelligence. These tasks include learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, understanding language, decision-making, and adapting to changing environments.

A widely accepted definition given by **John McCarthy (1956)**, who coined the term Artificial Intelligence, is:

>Artificial Intelligence is the science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs.

AI aims to create machines that can perceive their environment, process information, and take actions that maximize the likelihood of achieving specific goals.

## Objectives of AI

The primary objective of AI is not merely automation, but the development of **intelligent behavior** in machines.

Key objectives include:

- Learning from data and experience.
- Reasoning and drawing logical conclusions.
- Solving complex problems efficiently.
- Understanding natural language.
- Recognizing patterns, images, and speech.
- Making decisions under uncertainty.
- Interacting intelligently with humans and other systems.
- Adapting to new situations through learning.

## Characteristics of AI systems

An AI system generally possesses the following characteristics:

- **Perception**: ability to interpret sensory data such as images, sound, or text.
- **Knowledge representation**: storing information in a form that can be used for reasoning.
- **Reasoning**: deriving conclusions from available knowledge.
- **Learning**: improving performance based on experience or data.
- **Problem solving**: finding solutions through search and optimization techniques.
- **Autonomy**: operating without continuous human intervention.
- **Adaptability**: modifying behavior according to environmental changes.

## AI as intelligent behavior

Intelligence can be viewed as the ability to achieve goals in different environments. AI systems attempt to emulate this capability.

An intelligent system typically performs the following cycle:

**Intelligent Behavior Cycle**

1. Environment
2. Perception (Sensors)
3. Reasoning & Learning
4. Decision Making
5. Action (Actuators)

This perception–reasoning–action loop forms the foundation of intelligent agents studied later in the unit.

## Approaches to AI

AI researchers have traditionally viewed intelligence through four major approaches.

### Acting humanly (Turing test approach)

A machine is considered intelligent if it behaves like a human.

Requirements include:

- Natural language processing.
- Knowledge representation.
- Automated reasoning.
- Machine learning.

### Thinking humanly (cognitive modeling approach)

The machine should think in the same way humans think.

### Thinking rationally (laws of thought approach)

Intelligence is based on logical reasoning and correct inference.

### Acting rationally (rational agent approach)

An intelligent agent selects actions that maximize goal achievement. This is the **most widely used modern AI approach**.

## Types of AI

AI can be classified according to capability.

### Narrow AI (Weak AI)

Designed for a specific task.

Examples:

- Voice assistants.
- Recommendation systems.
- Spam filters.
- Face recognition.

### General AI (Strong AI)

A hypothetical AI capable of performing any intellectual task that a human can perform.

### Super AI

A theoretical form of AI that surpasses human intelligence in all domains.

## Advantages of AI

- High accuracy in repetitive tasks.
- Continuous operation without fatigue.
- Fast processing of large volumes of data.
- Improved decision support.
- Automation of hazardous tasks.
- Enhanced productivity and efficiency.

## Limitations of AI

- Lack of genuine understanding and consciousness.
- Dependence on quality and quantity of data.
- Limited common-sense reasoning.
- High computational and development costs.
- Ethical and privacy concerns.
- Potential bias in AI models.

## AI, machine learning, and deep learning

These terms are related but not identical.

**Relationship of AI, ML, and DL**

Artificial Intelligence ->
Machine Learning ->
Deep Learning

- **Artificial Intelligence**: broader field of creating intelligent systems.
- **Machine Learning**: subset of AI that learns from data.
- **Deep Learning**: subset of ML based on multi-layer neural networks.

## Key Points

**Artificial Intelligence**: The branch of computer science concerned with creating machines capable of intelligent behavior.

**Intelligent system**: A system that perceives its environment, reasons about it, learns from experience, and acts to achieve goals.

**Rational agent**: An entity that selects actions that maximize the expected achievement of its objectives.

## Conclusion

Artificial Intelligence is the discipline of building intelligent systems that can perceive, reason, learn, and act autonomously. Modern AI primarily follows the **rational agent approach**, where intelligent agents make decisions to achieve predefined goals efficiently. AI provides the theoretical foundation for search algorithms, intelligent agents, machine learning, and advanced decision-making techniques that are studied throughout this course.
