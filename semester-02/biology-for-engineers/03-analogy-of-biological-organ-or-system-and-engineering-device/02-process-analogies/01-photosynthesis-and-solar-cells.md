# Photosynthesis & Solar Cells

## 1. Introduction

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants, algae, and some microorganisms convert **light energy into chemical energy**.

A **solar cell (photovoltaic cell)** similarly converts **solar radiation into electrical energy**.

Thus, both systems demonstrate the fundamental engineering concept of:

> **Converting solar energy into a usable form of energy.**

## 2. Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis mainly occurs in the **chloroplasts** of plant cells. The pigment **chlorophyll** absorbs sunlight and provides the energy required for the process.

The overall reaction can be represented as:

$$
6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{Light energy}
\rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2
$$

### Inputs

* **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** - obtained from the atmosphere
* **Water (H₂O)** - absorbed mainly through roots
* **Light energy** - obtained from sunlight
* **Chlorophyll** - captures light energy

### Outputs

* **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)** - stores chemical energy
* **Oxygen (O₂)** - released as a by-product

### Basic energy conversion

$$
\boxed{\text{Solar energy} \rightarrow \text{Chemical energy}}
$$

The chemical energy is stored primarily in the bonds of glucose and other organic molecules.

## 3. Solar Cells

A **solar cell** is a semiconductor device that converts sunlight directly into electrical energy through the **photovoltaic effect**.

Most conventional solar cells are based on **semiconductor materials such as silicon**.

### Input

* Solar radiation

### Output

* Electrical energy

### Basic energy conversion

$$
\boxed{\text{Solar energy} \rightarrow \text{Electrical energy}}
$$

When photons from sunlight strike the semiconductor, they transfer energy to electrons. This creates charge carriers, and the built-in electric field of the photovoltaic junction drives these charges, producing an **electric current** when an external circuit is connected.

## 4. Analogy Between Photosynthesis and Solar Cells

| Photosynthesis | Solar Cell |
| --- | --- |
| Occurs in chloroplasts | Occurs in semiconductor photovoltaic cells |
| Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight | Semiconductor absorbs photons |
| Sunlight provides energy | Sunlight provides energy |
| Water and CO₂ are raw materials | Semiconductor generates charge carriers |
| Light energy is converted into chemical energy | Light energy is converted into electrical energy |
| Glucose stores the captured energy | Electrical circuit delivers usable electrical energy |
| Oxygen is released as a by-product | No comparable chemical by-product is produced |
| Biological energy-conversion system | Engineered energy-conversion system |

### Key analogy

$$
\boxed{
\text{Sunlight}
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{Photosynthesis} \rightarrow \text{Chemical energy}\\
\text{Solar cell} \rightarrow \text{Electrical energy}
\end{cases}
}
$$

## 5. Step-by-Step Comparison

### In Photosynthesis

**Step 1:** Sunlight reaches the leaf.

**Step 2:** Chlorophyll absorbs photons.

**Step 3:** The absorbed light energy drives a series of biochemical reactions.

**Step 4:** Water and carbon dioxide are processed through these reactions.

**Step 5:** Energy-rich organic molecules such as glucose are produced.

**Result:**

$$
\text{Light energy} \rightarrow \text{Chemical energy}
$$

### In a Solar Cell

**Step 1:** Sunlight reaches the photovoltaic material.

**Step 2:** Photons are absorbed by the semiconductor.

**Step 3:** Absorbed photon energy excites electrons.

**Step 4:** The photovoltaic junction separates and directs charge carriers.

**Step 5:** Current flows through an external circuit.

**Result:**

$$
\text{Light energy} \rightarrow \text{Electrical energy}
$$

## 6. Important Similarities

### 1. Same primary energy source

Both systems use **solar radiation** as their primary energy input.

### 2. Light absorption

Both depend on a material capable of **absorbing photons**.

* Photosynthesis → **chlorophyll**
* Solar cell → **semiconductor material**

### 3. Energy conversion

Both convert relatively inaccessible solar energy into a form that can be used or stored.

### 4. Complex internal mechanisms

Neither process is simply "sunlight goes in and energy comes out." Both involve multiple microscopic processes that control energy conversion.

### 5. Dependence on light intensity

Generally, increasing available light can increase the energy-conversion rate, although the response eventually becomes limited by other factors.

## 7. Important Differences

| Feature | Photosynthesis | Solar Cell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Biological | Engineered |
| Main light absorber | Chlorophyll and associated pigments | Semiconductor |
| Main product | Chemical energy/organic compounds | Electrical energy |
| Main raw materials | CO₂ and H₂O | Primarily incident sunlight |
| Energy storage | Chemical bonds | Can be supplied to a load or stored separately |
| By-product | O₂ | No oxygen production |
| Operating mechanism | Biochemical reactions | Photovoltaic/electronic processes |
| Self-maintaining system | Yes, as part of a living organism | No |
| Efficiency limitations | Biological and environmental factors | Material, optical, electrical and thermal losses |

## 8. Engineering Significance

The comparison between photosynthesis and solar cells demonstrates an important principle in **biomimicry and bio-inspired engineering**.

Scientists and engineers study natural energy-conversion systems to understand how biological systems:

* capture energy efficiently,
* operate under variable environmental conditions,
* organize complex processes,
* repair or replace damaged components, and
* convert energy using abundant natural resources.

This knowledge can inspire the development of **artificial photosynthesis**, improved photovoltaic materials, and other sustainable-energy technologies.

## Key Points

> **Photosynthesis:** Biological conversion of solar energy into chemical energy.

> **Solar cell:** Engineered conversion of solar energy into electrical energy using the photovoltaic effect.

> **Main similarity:** Both capture energy from sunlight.

> **Main difference:** Photosynthesis stores energy mainly as **chemical energy**, whereas solar cells produce **electrical energy**.

> **Biological absorber:** Chlorophyll.

> **Solar-cell absorber:** Semiconductor material, commonly silicon.

> **Engineering connection:** Photosynthesis provides a model for **solar-energy harvesting and bio-inspired energy conversion**.
