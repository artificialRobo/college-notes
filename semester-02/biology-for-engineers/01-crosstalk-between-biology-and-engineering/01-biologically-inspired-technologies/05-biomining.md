# Biomining

## 1. Introduction

**Biomining** is a technique in which **microorganisms are used to extract valuable metals from ores or mineral-containing materials**.

It is a biological approach to metal extraction and is an important example of how **biology and engineering can work together**.

Traditional metal extraction often relies on processes that require high temperatures, large amounts of energy, or strong chemical reagents. Biomining uses the metabolic activities of microorganisms to help **dissolve, mobilize, or concentrate metals**.

> **Key idea:** *Biomining uses microorganisms to help recover metals from ores and other mineral resources.*

---

## 2. Definition

> **Biomining is the extraction or recovery of metals from ores using microorganisms and their biological activities.**

The microorganisms involved are generally capable of obtaining energy by oxidizing **inorganic substances such as iron or sulfur compounds**.

Their activities alter the chemical environment of the ore and facilitate the release of metals.

---

# 3. Why Is Biomining Needed?

Many ores contain valuable metals in relatively low concentrations.

Conventional extraction methods may become:

* energy-intensive,
* expensive,
* environmentally demanding, or
* unsuitable for certain low-grade ores.

Biomining provides an alternative approach that can be particularly useful for **low-grade ores and ores that are difficult to process using conventional methods**.

---

# 4. Microorganisms Used in Biomining

Several microorganisms can participate in biomining processes.

Important examples include bacteria and archaea capable of oxidizing iron and/or sulfur compounds.

### Commonly studied microorganisms

* **Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans**
* **Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans**
* **Leptospirillum** species
* Certain **thermophilic archaea**

These microorganisms are generally adapted to **acidic and metal-rich environments**.

---

# 5. Basic Principle of Biomining

The basic process can be simplified as:

**Ore containing metal**
↓
**Microorganisms interact with mineral components**
↓
**Iron and/or sulfur compounds are oxidized**
↓
**Mineral structure is chemically altered**
↓
**Metal is released into solution**
↓
**Metal is recovered from the solution**

The microorganisms therefore act as **biological catalysts** that accelerate reactions involved in metal extraction.

---

# 6. Role of Microorganisms

Microorganisms can contribute to biomining mainly through the oxidation of **iron and sulfur-containing minerals**.

For example, microorganisms can oxidize ferrous iron:

$$
Fe^{2+} \rightarrow Fe^{3+}
$$

The resulting ferric iron can act as an oxidizing agent and attack sulfide minerals.

Microorganisms can also oxidize reduced sulfur compounds, producing sulfuric acid under suitable conditions.

These reactions help convert metals from relatively insoluble mineral forms into **soluble forms** that can subsequently be recovered.

---

# 7. Bioleaching

A major biomining process is called **bioleaching**.

### Definition

> **Bioleaching is the process of using microorganisms to convert metals present in insoluble minerals into soluble forms, allowing the metals to be recovered from the resulting solution.**

For example, microorganisms can help release metals such as:

* Copper
* Nickel
* Zinc
* Cobalt
* Uranium

from appropriate ores or mineral wastes.

---

# 8. Example: Copper Bioleaching

Copper sulfide ores can be processed using microorganisms capable of oxidizing iron and sulfur compounds.

### Simplified process

**Copper sulfide ore**
↓
**Microbial oxidation**
↓
**Copper becomes soluble**
↓
**Copper-rich solution (leachate)**
↓
**Copper recovery**

The copper can then be recovered from the solution using downstream processes such as **solvent extraction and electrowinning**, depending on the specific process.

---

# 9. Types of Biomining

Biomining is commonly discussed in terms of processes such as **bioleaching** and **biooxidation**.

### 9.1 Bioleaching

Microorganisms help **solubilize the target metal**, allowing it to enter an aqueous solution.

**Example:** Recovery of copper from suitable sulfide ores.

### 9.2 Biooxidation

Microorganisms oxidize mineral components associated with a valuable metal, making the valuable metal more accessible for subsequent extraction.

This is particularly important in the processing of certain **refractory gold ores**.

> **Difference:** In bioleaching, the target metal is generally transferred into solution; in biooxidation, microbial oxidation primarily breaks down the surrounding mineral matrix to make the valuable metal accessible.

---

# 10. Steps in a Typical Biomining Process

### Step 1: Ore preparation

The ore is crushed and prepared to increase the surface area available for microbial activity.

### Step 2: Microbial treatment

The prepared ore is exposed to suitable microorganisms under controlled conditions.

### Step 3: Oxidation of minerals

Microorganisms oxidize iron and/or sulfur compounds associated with the mineral.

### Step 4: Metal dissolution

The reactions facilitate the conversion of the target metal into a soluble form.

### Step 5: Collection of leachate

The metal-containing solution is collected.

### Step 6: Metal recovery

The desired metal is separated and recovered from the solution using appropriate chemical or electrochemical methods.

---

# 11. Methods of Biomining

Biomining can be carried out using different engineering configurations.

### 11.1 Heap Bioleaching

Crushed ore is arranged in large heaps and irrigated with a suitable acidic solution.

The solution passes through the ore while microorganisms promote mineral oxidation.

**Advantages:**

* Suitable for large quantities of low-grade ore
* Relatively simple infrastructure
* Lower energy requirements than some conventional methods

---

### 11.2 Dump Bioleaching

Low-grade or waste material is accumulated in large dumps and treated with leaching solutions.

Microbial activity gradually helps mobilize the desired metals.

It is generally a **slow process**, but it can be economical for certain low-grade resources.

---

### 11.3 Stirred-Tank Bioleaching

Finely ground ore is mixed with microorganisms and leaching solution inside specially designed reactors.

Compared with heap processes, stirred tanks allow better control of:

* temperature,
* pH,
* oxygen supply,
* mixing, and
* microbial growth.

However, they generally require greater capital and operating costs.

---

# 12. Advantages of Biomining

### 12.1 Lower Energy Requirement

Biomining can operate at relatively moderate temperatures compared with energy-intensive high-temperature metal extraction processes.

### 12.2 Suitable for Low-Grade Ores

Microbial processes can make the recovery of metals from certain low-grade ores economically feasible.

### 12.3 Reduced Need for Harsh Processing

In suitable applications, biomining can reduce reliance on some energy-intensive or chemically intensive extraction steps.

### 12.4 Economical for Certain Resources

For appropriate ores and operating conditions, biomining can provide an economically attractive extraction route.

### 12.5 Recovery from Waste Materials

Microbial processes can potentially be applied to **mine wastes, tailings, and other secondary resources** containing valuable metals.

---

# 13. Limitations of Biomining

Biomining also has important limitations.

### 13.1 Slow Process

Microbial metal extraction can be considerably slower than some conventional extraction processes.

### 13.2 Requires Specific Conditions

Microorganisms require suitable:

* temperature,
* pH,
* oxygen,
* nutrients, and
* moisture.

### 13.3 Not Suitable for Every Ore

The effectiveness depends strongly on the **mineralogy and composition of the ore**.

### 13.4 Environmental Concerns

Although biomining can reduce certain environmental impacts, acidic metal-containing solutions can pose environmental risks if they are not properly controlled.

### 13.5 Process Control

Large-scale biomining requires careful monitoring of microbial activity and chemical conditions.

---

# 14. Biomining vs. Conventional Mining

| Feature              | Conventional extraction       | Biomining                                               |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Main agent           | Physical/chemical processes   | Microorganisms + chemical reactions                     |
| Temperature          | May involve high temperatures | Often moderate                                          |
| Energy requirement   | Can be high                   | Generally lower for suitable processes                  |
| Processing speed     | Often faster                  | Often slower                                            |
| Low-grade ores       | May be uneconomical           | Can be suitable in some cases                           |
| Environmental impact | Can be significant            | Can reduce some impacts but requires careful management |
| Biological component | Not essential                 | Essential                                               |

---

# 15. Applications of Biomining

Biomining has been applied or investigated for recovery of several metals, including:

* **Copper**
* **Gold**
* **Uranium**
* **Nickel**
* **Cobalt**
* **Zinc**

It is particularly important in the processing of **sulfide minerals** and certain difficult-to-process ores.

---

# 16. Biomining and Sustainability

Biomining is considered a potentially more sustainable approach because microorganisms can perform useful chemical transformations under relatively mild conditions.

It can contribute to:

* resource recovery,
* utilization of low-grade ores,
* recovery of metals from mining waste,
* reduced energy consumption in suitable processes, and
* more efficient utilization of mineral resources.

However, **biomining is not automatically environmentally harmless**. Acidic solutions and dissolved metals must be carefully managed to prevent environmental contamination.

---

# 17. Connection Between Biology and Engineering

Biomining is a clear example of **engineering exploiting biological activity**.

### Biology provides:

* microorganisms,
* metabolic pathways,
* catalytic activity,
* adaptation to extreme environments.

### Engineering provides:

* bioreactors,
* heap systems,
* aeration,
* temperature and pH control,
* leachate collection,
* metal recovery systems.

Therefore:

> **Biomining = Microbial activity + Chemical engineering + Mineral processing**

---

# 18. Key Terms

**Biomining:**
Extraction or recovery of metals using microorganisms.

**Bioleaching:**
Microbial conversion of metals in minerals into soluble forms for recovery.

**Biooxidation:**
Microbial oxidation of mineral components to make a valuable metal more accessible for subsequent extraction.

**Leachate:**
The liquid solution containing dissolved substances, including metals, obtained during leaching.

**Low-grade ore:**
Ore containing a relatively small concentration of the desired valuable mineral or metal.

# 19. Exam-Oriented Answer

> **Biomining is a biological technique for extracting metals from ores using microorganisms. Microorganisms such as *Acidithiobacillus* and *Leptospirillum* can oxidize iron and sulfur compounds present in mineral ores. These reactions alter the mineral structure and help convert valuable metals into soluble forms. The metal-containing solution is then collected and processed to recover the desired metal. Two important biomining processes are bioleaching, in which the target metal is solubilized, and biooxidation, in which surrounding minerals are oxidized to make the valuable metal accessible. Biomining can be advantageous for low-grade ores because it may require less energy than some conventional extraction methods, although it is generally slower and requires careful control of environmental conditions.**
